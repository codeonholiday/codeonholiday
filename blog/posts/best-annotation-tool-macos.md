If you teach online, present software, run workshops, or record tutorials, the best annotation tool for macOS should do more than draw a quick arrow. It should help people follow your cursor, understand a changing interface, pause a complicated moment, and keep the explanation moving while you share your screen.

This guide compares **HoverBoard**, **Presentify**, **ScreenBrush**, and the annotation tools built into Zoom. The short version is simple: Presentify and ScreenBrush are good focused overlays, and Zoom is convenient when you only need to annotate inside a call. HoverBoard is the strongest all-round **screen annotation app for Mac** because it adds Freeze, Spotlight, free **Screenshot** and **Screen Record**, Cursor Halo, Keystroke Display, Whiteboard, Notes, Sessions, and local meeting recording around the core drawing workflow.

## What makes the best annotation tool for Mac?

Before comparing apps, it helps to separate four common needs:

- **Draw on screen:** arrows, highlights, shapes, text, and freehand marks over Keynote, PowerPoint, a browser, an IDE, or a video call
- **Direct attention:** spotlight an area, make the cursor visible, or show clicks and keystrokes
- **Explain a moment:** freeze a live screen, open a whiteboard, or keep a private presenter note visible only to you
- **Screenshot and screen record:** capture stills or MP4 while presenting — both free forever in HoverBoard 1.4

Many Mac annotation apps solve the first need. The important difference is what happens when drawing is not enough.

[HoverBoard](/hoverboard/) is a native macOS menu bar app for presenting, teaching, screen sharing, and software demos. Press a hotkey and draw over whatever is already on your screen. You can use a pen, highlighter, arrows, rectangles, text, laser pointer, and configurable colors and stroke widths.

Drawings can auto-fade or stay visible until you dismiss them. That makes HoverBoard useful for both quick emphasis during a live explanation and step-by-step walkthroughs where marks need to remain on screen.

The difference is workflow. HoverBoard is not only a tool to mark the screen; it is a set of presentation tools that stays available while you teach or explain.

## Presentify: best focused screen annotation app for Mac

[Presentify](https://presentifyapp.com/) is a focused Mac presentation overlay for drawing, cursor highlighting, spotlight, and zoom. It is a good choice when you want a lightweight way to annotate slides, code, videos, PDFs, or any other visible screen.

Presentify keeps the product scope clear. It works over other applications and gives presenters the essential visual tools without asking them to move their audience to another whiteboard or workspace. For a user who already has separate apps for notes, timers, recording, and exports, that simplicity can be an advantage.

### Where HoverBoard has an edge over Presentify

HoverBoard covers the same core annotation moments, then adds the tools around them: Freeze & Explain, a more complete Whiteboard, Cursor Halo, Keystroke Display, private Notes, Sessions, Break Timer, and local recording workflows.

That makes HoverBoard a useful **Presentify alternative** for teachers, trainers, developers, and creators who want to keep the entire explanation in one menu bar app. Presentify remains the better fit if you only want a small, dedicated overlay.

## ScreenBrush: a simple way to draw over the screen

ScreenBrush represents the simpler end of the Mac annotation category: activate the tool, draw over the current screen, and make a visual point while presenting or screen sharing.

This kind of utility is valuable when the requirement is narrow. You may not need a whiteboard, recording, or presenter notes for a five-minute demo. A compact drawing overlay can be faster to learn and easier to keep out of the way.

### Where HoverBoard has an edge over ScreenBrush

HoverBoard is designed for the next question that usually follows a mark on screen: “How do I explain this clearly and keep the useful result?” Freeze lets you stop a live interface from changing. Whiteboard gives you a separate canvas. Notes keeps private context beside the call. Sessions lets you save the important frames instead of losing the explanation when the meeting ends.

If you only want a digital pen, ScreenBrush may be enough. If you want a **Mac annotation tool for teaching, presenting, and screen recording**, HoverBoard provides a broader workflow.

## Zoom annotation: convenient inside a meeting, limited outside it

Zoom includes annotation tools while screen sharing, including drawing, text, and shapes. That is convenient when you are already in a Zoom call and only need to mark the shared screen for participants.

The limitation is that Zoom annotation is tied to the meeting and its sharing workflow. It is not a general presentation overlay for Keynote, PowerPoint, browsers, IDEs, recordings, or Google Meet. You also may not want to expose every presenter tool to meeting participants.

HoverBoard works over the applications you already use and is independent of the video-call provider. That means the same hotkeys and visual language work in Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, a classroom, a local recording, or an in-person presentation.
