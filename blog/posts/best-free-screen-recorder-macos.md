QuickTime Recorder is free but basic. [Loom](https://www.loom.com/) and [Screen Studio](https://www.screen.studio/) optimize for polished async video. [HoverBoard](/hoverboard/) 1.4 adds **Screen Record free forever** — built for people who record while they teach, demo, or annotate live.

## What to look for in a Mac screen recorder

- **Capture modes** — full display, region, or a single window
- **Audio** — microphone and system sound toggles
- **Does not block your flow** — draw or spotlight while recording
- **Edit before share** — trim dead air, speed up, crop
- **Local files** — MP4 on disk, no required cloud account

## HoverBoard Screen Record — free forever

Press **⌃⌥V** to open the mode chooser:

- **Full Screen** — record the display under your pointer
- **Selection** — drag a region
- **Window** — pick one window

A floating **record pill** shows timer, **mic** and **system audio** toggles, and **Finish** (the only way to stop). After Finish, trim with Keep / Split / Delete, change **speed up to 10×**, crop, then **Save** or **Save As** to `~/Movies/HoverBoard Recordings/`.

Screen Record does **not** start the Pro trial. It is separate from [Notes meeting recording](/blog/how-to-use-hoverboard-recording/) (transcription and summaries — Pro workflow).

### Honest limits

HoverBoard is **not** Loom or Screen Studio. No instant share links, no cinematic auto-zoom, no team video library. For async polish, record with HoverBoard live, then edit in Screen Studio — see [Loom / Screen Studio alternative](/blog/loom-screen-studio-alternative-macos/).

## QuickTime Player — free and built in

QuickTime records screen and audio with minimal setup. Editing is limited; trimming often means another app. Fine for quick clips; thin for teaching workflows with overlay tools.

## Loom / Screen Studio — best for shareable async video

**Loom** excels at record → link → comment. **Screen Studio** excels at beautiful cursor motion and creator-grade exports. Choose them when the deliverable is a hosted or heavily edited video, not a live annotated demo.
