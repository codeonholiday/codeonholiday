On a shared screen, a normal pointer can disappear against a bright slide, a white web page, or a dense IDE. Cursor Halo adds a visible ring around the pointer and can show a click ripple so viewers understand exactly where you are working.

## Activate Cursor Halo

The default shortcut is **⌃⌥H**. Open it from the menu bar or Settings if you prefer. Cursor Halo is a Pro feature with a 60-second trial each time you open it; Pro unlocks unlimited use.

Once active, move the pointer normally. The halo follows it without changing the pointer’s behavior, so you can still click and interact with the underlying app. Press **Esc** to close the overlay.

## Tune the visual signal

Cursor Halo settings let you adjust the things viewers notice most:

- **Color:** choose a color that contrasts with your slides and recording background.
- **Size:** use a larger halo for remote teaching or a small one for a product demo.
- **Animation:** choose how the halo appears or responds to movement.
- **Click effect:** show a ripple when you click so an interaction is unmistakable.

Use a calm, high-contrast color for a long class. A brighter click ripple works well in a short tutorial where viewers need to replay each action.

## Make cursor movement easier to read

Cursor Halo works best when paired with intentional movement:

1. Activate the halo before sharing the screen.
2. Move the pointer slowly to the area you are about to discuss.
3. Pause briefly before clicking.
4. Click once and let the ripple communicate the action.
5. Avoid circling the whole screen; use Draw or Spotlight when the audience needs a larger emphasis.

The goal is not to decorate the cursor. It is to give viewers a stable visual anchor.

## Halo versus Spotlight

Use **Cursor Halo** when the whole screen should remain visible and you want to make pointer movement legible. Use [Spotlight](/blog/how-to-use-hoverboard-spotlight/) when the audience should ignore everything except one area. Use [Draw](/blog/how-to-use-hoverboard-draw/) when a persistent mark, arrow, or shape explains the relationship.

## Restore defaults

If experimentation leaves the halo too large, too bright, or too animated, open its settings panel and choose **Restore Defaults**. Recent versions also dismiss settings when you click outside the panel, so changing a value does not require hunting for the gear button.

## Permissions and privacy

Cursor Halo is a local visual overlay. It does not record or upload your pointer movement. Global activation requires Accessibility permission so HoverBoard can receive its shortcut; the halo itself does not need an account or cloud service.

## Troubleshooting

If the halo does not appear, confirm that the mode is active and that HoverBoard is allowed under Accessibility. Check whether a second monitor is active and move the pointer there; the overlay follows the display containing the cursor. If the click ripple is hard to see, increase its size or choose a higher-contrast color in settings.

Cursor Halo is a small feature with a large effect in remote presentations: viewers stop asking “where?” and can follow the demo on the first try.
